A personal organization is for your own work and has no members, roles or grants. An external
organization has members, and roles give them organization-wide permissions, and **View**, **Use**,
**Manage** or **Admin** on selected folders, grants, service keys and provider credentials. A
[grant](/docs/grants) controls which member keys a person can create.

## Give a teammate API access

1. Choose or create a [grant](/docs/grants) with suitable limits.
2. Choose or create a role with **Use** on that grant or its folder. It needs no organization-wide
   API key permissions.
3. Invite the teammate and assign the role. Their default role and any other roles also apply.
4. The teammate creates their own key under the grant.

Inviting and assigning roles needs **Members → Manage**. Assigning a role that gives grant access
also needs **Admin** on that grant, its folder or the organization. Creating or editing a role needs
**Roles → Manage**.

## Invitations

Deleting an invitation stops further joins, and members who already joined stay. A
[single sign-on](/docs/single-sign-on) invite has no link: people at your verified domains join
through it when they sign in through your identity provider.

## Pictures

A member's picture comes from your [identity provider](/docs/single-sign-on) until they upload one,
and a removed picture stays removed until the member uploads another.

Stogas keeps only a square image of at most 128 pixels a side, encoded again from the image's
pixels, so no metadata such as a camera's location stays with it.

## Roles

- A member gets the combined access of all their roles.
- Members can assign organization-wide permissions they hold. Giving new access to API keys or
  credentials also needs **Admin** on the matching resource.
- Taking a role from someone, or removing them, needs the same permissions as assigning that role.
  You can drop your own roles.
- **Roles → Manage** can create roles with permissions the manager does not hold, so give it only
  to people you trust with full control.

## Remove access

Removing a member's last **Use** on a grant deletes their keys under it, as
[Grants](/docs/grants) explains. To pause access without deleting anything, disable the grant or
the key instead. Removing someone's access to a service key keeps the key; rotate its secret to
stop copies they may hold.

A member's invitations stay valid after they leave. When the last member leaves, the organization
is deleted, and its financial history is kept.

## Permission reference

### Resource permissions

Add a folder, grant, service key or provider credential to a role and choose:

| Permission | Allows                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **View**   | Read settings, policies and usage.                                                               |
| **Use**    | View, plus reveal a service-key secret, create member keys from a grant, or assign a credential. |
| **Manage** | View, plus configure, rotate, disable or delete the resource.                                    |
| **Admin**  | Use and Manage, plus give others access within that resource.                                    |

Use and Manage can be combined as **Use and manage**. Manage alone cannot reveal a secret or give
access to someone else.

- Only the owner can see a member key's secret and send requests with it. Managers can change its
  settings.
- Credentials are not in folders. To add one to a key, see
  [Order credentials](/docs/byok#order-credentials).

Organization permissions, such as **Members → Manage**, work across the organization, and the role
editor describes each one. Permissions decide what a member can do;
[policies](/docs/policies/scopes) on their roles can still narrow their requests.
[Who can edit each policy](/docs/policies/editing#who-can-edit) also depends on the parent
policy's delegation settings.
