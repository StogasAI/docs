---
title: Single sign-on
description: Sign members in through your identity provider, and let people at your domains join.
icon: LogIn
---

An external organization can connect an OpenID Connect identity provider, such as Okta, Microsoft
Entra ID or Google Workspace. Members then sign in through it, the organization can require that,
and people at its verified domains can join through a single sign-on invite. SAML isn't supported.
To set it up, open **Settings → Single sign-on** and select **Set up**. This needs
**Organization → Manage**.

## Verify a domain

Add the domain, publish the TXT record that **Settings** shows, then select **Verify**:

| Name                         | Value     |
| ---------------------------- | --------- |
| `_stogas-challenge.<domain>` | `<token>` |

The value can also be `token=<token>`. A domain stays verified, so you can remove the record once
it is. When another organization verifies the same domain with its own record, the domain moves to
that organization. Public suffixes such as `co.uk` or `github.io`, and IP addresses, can't be
verified. An organization verifies up to 10 domains.

## Connect an identity provider

<Steps>
<Step>

### Register Stogas with the provider

Create a web application with the authorization code flow. Use the redirect URI that **Settings**
shows, `https://app.stogas.ai/auth/sso/callback`. Sign-in asks only for the `openid` and `email`
scopes.

</Step>
<Step>

### Enter the issuer, client ID and client secret

The issuer's OpenID Connect discovery document must name exactly that issuer. A multi-tenant
endpoint, such as Microsoft's `common`, lets any tenant sign in, so it is refused. For Microsoft
Entra ID, use `https://login.microsoftonline.com/<tenant-id>/v2.0`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Test the sign-in

**Test sign-in** signs you in through the provider and links your account. Members can sign in
through the provider after a test succeeds.

</Step>
</Steps>

A new issuer or client ID needs a new test, and turns **Require single sign-on** off until then. A
new issuer also unlinks every member, because its people are not the old provider's. Members link
by the provider's subject identifier, so a provider that gives each client its own identifiers
keeps the links only while the client ID stays the same.

## Let people join

People join through the organization's single sign-on invite. Once a domain is verified and the
sign-in is tested, create it in **Members → Invites** with the type **Single sign-on**, and set how
many people can join, up to 1,000. Like any invite, each unused place holds a seat, and the invite
is deleted when its places run out. An organization has one single sign-on invite at a time.

A person joins when the provider signs them in with a verified email address at one of your
verified domains and the invite has a place left. Their name in the organization starts as that
email address.

## Sign in

On the sign-in page, people select **SSO** and enter their work email. After the
provider signs them in:

- A person whose account is linked is signed in to it. If the account has a passkey or an
  authenticator app, that step follows.
- Anyone else chooses an account. **Create an account** joins the organization with a new account
  and shows its password once. **Use an existing account** signs in to an account and links it. An
  account that isn't a member yet joins through the invite.

Every account has a password, including accounts created through single sign-on. Members can always
sign in with it, so leaving the organization or disconnecting the provider never locks anyone out.

## Require single sign-on

With **Require single sign-on** on, members must sign in through the provider every 24 hours to
open the organization in the dashboard.

- Members who manage the organization are exempt, so a broken connection can't lock everyone out.
- Access tokens and API keys aren't affected, because programs use them. To refuse access tokens,
  turn off **Allow access tokens** in **Settings**.
- A member can always leave the organization.
- Turning it on applies at once, also to members who are already signed in.

## Accounts and identities

An account links to one person at the provider in each organization, and that person to one
account. Links use the provider's subject identifier, never the email, which some providers let
people change. **Members** marks the members who use single sign-on, and the `sso:true` filter
lists them. Leaving the organization removes the member's link. Disconnecting the provider removes
every link and the single sign-on invite.

Stogas stores the issuer, the client ID, the client secret encrypted, and each linked member's
subject identifier and last sign-in. [Activity](/docs/activity) records changes to the connection,
the setting, the domains and the invite, never the secret. It also records each member who joins
through single sign-on or links an account to it.
